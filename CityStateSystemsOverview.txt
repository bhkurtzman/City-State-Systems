Reference this Guide for context regarding the long-term goals of CityStateSystems

Goals:
CityStateSystems is a working title for a strategy game prototype. My main goals are creating systems that work together and can be ported to a future desktop application.
 The game puts the user in charge of a newly founded micro-nation with a unique people. The goal is to create an accurate demographic, economic, cultural, logistical, and urbanist simulator.
 The player will be given a suite of statistics and policy nobs to create whatever kind of legal system, culture, and economy he wants.
 That said, the goal is for the simulator to work as a kind of ant farm, where the game can just run without much user input due to the dynamic and balanced systems.
 Realism is the goal: all citizen behavior, production and consumption, and economic activity should be based on reality.
 My medium term goal is for this grid/turn based game to run on a map-size of about 1000km2 with up to 100k population. Long term goal for eventual desktop app is 20,000km2 and up to 10m population.
 The game will also have a tech progress element. Your nation will research technologies at a variable pace which will unlock new buildings, efficiencies, resources, and policies.
 The game follows a simple philosophy that base extraction and especially agriculture are the foundation of civilization-- as a nation advances, agriculture becomes more efficient so more people may specialize.
 A key mechanic that is rather unique: demographics will play a big role-- Vigor, Intellect, and Willpower are heritable and marriage and fertility are dependant on the economy, culture, and laws of your nation. 
 Contrary to most city builders, increasing population through migration will be more difficult and have trade-offs concerning immigrant culture and attributes.
 A users country will be measured relative to other regions, which will follow a static progress and development schedule based on history. Depending on player decisions, your nation can be advanced or fall behind. Developed or undeveloped.
 
 Current State:
 This is a turn based game. Each turn represents one month. The map is 2d with 10mx10m tiles.
 The map and buildings on the map are also tracked-- the map has terrain features that influence buildings.
 Climate and map generation are set by the user. Climate affects map generation and agriculture.
 Each citizen is a unique agent with attributes, relationships, and information kept in a ledger that updates once per month.
 A citizen has parents listed. Siblings are citizens who share at least one parent.
 Siblings may not marry. A dead citizen is removed from the ledger once he has no living relatives (children, siblings, spouse). The ledger is rewritten when dead citizens are removed to maintain citizen paths.
 Citizens have 3 attributes that are partially heritable (genetically). Vigor, Intellect, Willpower. Each citizen has either level 1, 2, or 3 for each of these skills. Higher levels are typically better and bonuses are given to various probabilities depending on level.
 The basic unit is the family. Families form when a man and woman get married. A husband is the head of the family and keeps the family wealth in his purse. While a father is alive and under 65, his eldest son, that son's wife, and their children stay in his household. Children under 18 live with their family unless the parents have died. A younger son leaves when he takes a plot of his own.
 The game has three starting conditions, Warrior, Merchant, Explorer. Each have a unique attribute bonus, starting number of families, and treasury.
 A family (or single adult) will occupy a building and run it as an owner/occupier.
 A shack is a 1×2 house on prairie, scrub, or barren ground, and not on a mountain. It produces nothing. The click uses the cell to the east, or the cell to the south when the east pair is illegal. The household that owns it is unemployed and still buys food, fuel, clothing, and leather. A shack is priced as two urban tiles, offered after the other plots, and uses the small-farm purse rule.
 A construction office is a 3×3 state building. The player sets 0–10 laborers, in steps of 2. Each laborer supplies 100 man-hours a month, and unused hours are not kept. Each month it hires unemployed men aged 18–55 until the jobs are filled. Men already hired may stay past 55. A cut fires the newest hires the next January, after the December E1 wage is paid from the treasury. E1, E2, and E3 wages are set in the Government menu. A laborer is E1. The office keeps 0.1 t of tools and 0.1 t of leather, buying the difference in November when it is short. In December, an office that worked that year rolls once per employee pair: 10% to use 0.1 t of tools and 5% to use 0.1 t of leather. A cart path is a one-tile road drawn in a straight north, south, east, or west line. It cannot cross water, mountains, or a building. A connected stretch is one project. The free office that would finish soonest takes it, and a smaller crew wins a tie. A tile is 10 man-hours, 15 on a hill, plus 10 for scrub, 20 for woodland, 50 for forest, and 30 for wetland. Woodland and forest are cleared to prairie and yield the same wood as a farmstead's clearing cut, 2 t and 5 t, into the office pantry. Wetland stays wetland and takes 1 t of wood. The office buys wood when clearing and the pantry do not cover that. After the project is finished, a market or workshop can buy the leftover wood. The treasury pays for the office's purchases.
 Farmsteads settle at 2–2,000 tiles and feature a single house. A larger outline can be drawn and filled, and it is outlined in yellow until it is cut down. Farmstead map tile composition affects farmstead production decisions. Each tile owned by a farmstead has a monthly production.
 Markets buy and sell domestically, taking a 10% (minimum $1) profit on all sales.
 Each good has a set foriegn regional price. This price is currently only set for western europe. The set foreign price is how much $ is exchanged for a given good. It is the same price for imports and exports. Any trading fees are separate.
 Trading posts import and export goods, 1 ton of goods each way per month. They rank a good by spare tons divided by the tons used in a year, counting a year that uses none as one ton. A good is offered only when that score is above 1.1 and the foreign price clears the fee. The roll is 80% for the highest score, 10% for the second, and 10% for any other offered good. The ton comes from one farmstead or workshop that has at least a ton to spare, chosen at random. If that good has no such ton, the post tries the next surplus goods, up to five. The seller is paid the foreign price minus the fee, and the post is paid the fee. A post takes one ton of grain in November. 
 Trading posts collect a fee for imports and exports by subtracting a dollar amount from purchases for export and adding the same fee to imported goods that they sell. Trading post fee is calculated based on the cost to purchase supplies needed to run the post and support their family.
 Producers will not export their goods if the trading post fee exceeds the foreign value of the good, therefore some goods are not exported.
 Workshops are primitive industrial buildings that specialize in producing one or a few similar types of goods. They require input resources, often fuel, and supplies. They produce a certain amount of product based on a known recipe.
 Each person living in a building requires supplies to survive. Various foods, fuel, and some durable goods are needed. The cost of living is an estimate of this basket of goods.
 A household's cost of living is counted once in full. Every other person adds food, clothing, and leather at three quarters of the adult rate. The full cost is food, working-class clothing and leather, a year of wood or a quarter as much coal, and two urban tiles at the yearly lease or one twentieth of the sale price.
 All goods/resources are measured in metric tons. Buildings will not buy or sell less than 0.1T at a time.
 Buildings and households attempt to hold at least 2 years of goods needed for survival.
 Buildings can buy resources the same turn (month) they are needed.
 Citizens have a chance of illness or injury and chance of death from said illness or injury that rolls each month. Those chances change with citizen age and are affected by their traits.
 Education is currently 0,1,2. Illiterate, Primary, Secondary. Schools are not implemented yet but a set of rules for homeschool (passive) chance and graduation is already laid out.
 Buildings/property are placed by the user. The user can set a yearly lease or sale price per tile of a specific type. Once a building is leased, that lease price is maintained. A lease is generally multiplied by 20 to get an equivalent sale price. This is useful because a farmstead that contains multiple types of tiles can only be leased or sold as a whole.
 Vacant plots go to the wealthiest eligible buyer, one plot each. Buyers are household heads without a plot, and landless men of 18 who are not students and are not the eldest son still in his father's house. A small farmstead or a shack uses the old affordability rule, so a purse of $0 can take a plot priced at $0. A farmstead over 1,000 tiles, a market, a trading post, or a workshop also requires a purse of at least five times one adult's yearly cost of living. Shacks are offered after those plots.
 The UI has a top bar with map overlays on the left and Population, Education, Economy, and Treasury menus on the right. These have statistics as well as allow the user to set land prices.
 
 File Guide:
 The simulation lives in `src/lib`. The screen that calls it is `src/components/planner-app.tsx`. One Advance Month runs the steps in this order: `advancePeople`, `commitSettlement`, `settleHoldings`, `runConstruction`, `workFarms`, `tradeMonth`, `runRoads`.

People are columns of typed arrays. Money is whole dollars. Barn stock is hundredths of a ton. The same seed always rebuilds the same result through `hashSeed` and `mulberry32` in `src/lib/noise.ts`.

**Where to edit a mechanic**

- **Founding families, purses, and opening ages.** `foundPeople` in `src/lib/people.ts`. A warrior starts 20 families and adds $100 for each year past 18. An explorer starts 30 families and adds $60 for each year past 18. A merchant starts 10 families and adds $150 for each year past 25.
- **Birth, death, aging, marriage, inheritance, epidemics, and the June school credits.** `advancePeople` in `src/lib/people.ts`. An eldest son still at home, or a man who owns a plot, may marry. Never-married women 18–45 may marry from any house. A widow under 45 may remarry only while she is still the head; widowers do not. A wife’s purse moves onto her husband. From a father's 65th birthday, or at his death, an adult eldest son takes the property. Half the purse goes to that son and half to the other sons, or to the daughters if there is no younger son. A father with no son retires onto his eldest married daughter when his last daughter marries, and the plot is sold back to the treasury. In January, a working father splits up to 20% of his purse among landless younger sons of 16, keeping one year of household cost. A daughter's wedding draws one dowry of up to 10% on the same terms.
- **Occupation.** `assignOccupations` in `src/lib/people.ts`. Student, then the job of the oldest house the owner or spouse holds, otherwise Idle under 18 and Unemployed at 18. A shack's job is Unemployed. A man on a construction crew whose house left him unemployed is a Laborer.
- **Homeless families and homeless single adults.** `residenceCensus` in `src/lib/people.ts`. A house is a farmstead, shack, market, trading post, or workshop that is already in place.
- **Classes, household cost, and who counts as one income.** `classCensus` in `src/lib/people.ts`. A couple costs twice an adult’s cost of living. The eldest son still at home and his wife each add a full adult, and their children add a full adult from 18 or half before that. Other children at home, adopted children, and retired parents add half. A single adult costs one, plus those same additions.
- **The income number itself.** `openIncomeMonth` and `noteOperatingIncome` in `src/lib/people.ts`. Sales, fees, goods bought, and leases are recorded. Land purchases, inheritances, and marriage transfers are not. The cash is tagged in `credit` inside `src/lib/trade.ts`, and lease payments are tagged in `settleHoldings` inside `src/lib/land.ts`.
- **Cost of living for one adult.** `costOfLiving` in `src/lib/trade.ts`. Grain, the fruit and vegetable average, and a year of protein weighted 10% meat, 45% fish, and 45% dairy. Dairy is 0.4 t. Clothing and leather use the working-class amounts. A full year of fuel and two urban tiles are added once per household.
- **Land prices, sale or lease, who is offered a plot, and the yearly lease bill.** `settleHoldings` in `src/lib/land.ts`. One plot per buyer. For the first ten years after founding, a market needs $2,000 and a post, workshop, or large farm needs $1,000. After that, those plots need five years of one adult's cost of living. A shack is two urban tiles, needs no purse beyond the price, and is offered after the other plots. `plotSalePrice` is what the treasury pays to take a plot back. `monthlyCapital` is the trading-post fee’s land part, not a second rent.
- **Placing, deleting, and finishing buildings.** `applyBuild` and `commitSettlement` in `src/lib/buildings.ts`. Logging camps and mining camps are listed and disabled. A shack is a green 1×2, east of the click or south when that pair is illegal, on prairie, scrub, or barren, and not on a mountain. A construction office is a yellow 3×3 on dry ground. A new farmstead over 2,000 tiles is dropped at the month advance. An established farm keeps its tiles and drops only the planned addition that pushed it over 2,000.
- **Construction crews and public wages.** `runConstruction` in `src/lib/construction.ts`. The Government menu sets E1, E2, and E3. Laborers are E1, paid in December. Hiring age is 18–55. January fires the newest extras. The office pantry is created empty in `workFarms`.
- **Cart paths.** `paintRoad`, `assignOffices`, and `runRoads` in `src/lib/roads.ts`. A path is a straight cardinal line and cannot cross water, a mountain, or a building. One connected stretch is one project. Each laborer gives 100 man-hours a month. Hill is 15 hours, scrub adds 10, woodland 20, forest 50, and wetland 30. Clearing woodland yields 2 t and forest 5 t. Wetland takes 1 t of wood. November tops tools and leather up to 0.1 t. December may consume them. Leftover wood can be sold once the office has no unfinished path.
- **Crops, farm yields, salt, farm coal, dried fruit, and opening barn stocks.** `workFarms` in `src/lib/produce.ts`. The good list and labels are `GOODS` and `GOOD_LABEL` at the top of that file. Household diet size is `familyPortions`. A farmstead's April fish haul stops at 15 t. A garden draw is 40% fiber, 20% fruit, and 20% vegetables, and the climate crop takes the remaining 20% when it is one of those three.
- **Workshops.** The batch constants and `runShops` in `src/lib/trade.ts`. Sawmill, blacksmith, smokehouse, creamery, and cooper. A creamery turns 8 t of dairy, 1 t of wood or 0.25 t of coal, and 0.02 t of salt into 1 t of cheese in March, April, May, September, October, and November. Cheese exports at $350. A cooper turns 2 t of wood and 0.2 t of steel parts into 1 t of barrels. Barrels export at $400 and have no local use yet. A specialty is rolled once per workshop in `produce.ts`.
- **Exports, imports, the trading-post fee, markets, the two-year reserve, and town prices.** `tradeMonth` in `src/lib/trade.ts`. Regional prices are `WEST_EUROPE`. The fee is `postFee`. The export roll is `rankedExports` and `pickExport`. A good ranks by spare tons divided by yearly use, and a year that uses none counts as one ton. It is listed only when that score is above 1.1 and the foreign price clears the fee. A post tries up to five goods. Reserves are `stockTargets`. A household eats a year of food in the head's birth month. A farm eats a food it grows in that crop's harvest month and sells the rest. Fruit or vegetables is an even chance, or three to one toward the crop the farm grows, unless one costs more than ten times the other. Then the household takes the cheaper one. Food the household does not produce, and does not use in a workshop, is not kept. A remainder under 0.1 t is wasted. Clothing is a January roll for 0.1 t: poor 2%, working 4%, middle 5%, upper 7%, wealthy 10%. Leather for ages 13 and up is half that chance. Under 13, clothing is half and leather is not bought. A farm's leather and textiles are a 5% chance of 0.1 t per 1,000 dry tiles. A workshop rolls tools at 5% and does not roll leather. A creamery also rolls textiles at 5%, and a cooper uses 0.2 t of fiber a year. Each farmstead keeps 0.1 t of tools, textiles, and leather, and each workshop keeps 0.1 t of tools. A creamery also keeps 0.1 t of textiles and 0.1 t of salt. A market opens at half of each shelf target, rounded to the nearest 0.1 t. Its shelf includes 1 t of salt. A buyer takes the smallest whole tenth of a ton from a market, or the smallest whole ton from anyone else, that reaches its target and then stops. A farmstead sells a protein only when that farm produces it. A farmstead sells at least a ton to a workshop at the town price, and buys only from a market in lots of at least 0.1 t. A post exports one ton a month from a farmstead or workshop with at least a ton to spare, chosen at random, and is paid the fee. The seller receives the foreign price minus the fee. A post sells imports, a ton or more, to a market or a workshop. A market buys and sells with farms and workshops in lots of at least 0.1 t, at ten percent over what it paid. Meat, fish, grain, dairy, fruit, and vegetables are filled the month after harvest, unless the town quote is more than 100 times the substitute. Fruit pairs with vegetables. Meat, fish, and dairy each pair with the cheaper of the other two. Later in the year a market buys only enough of those to cover a buyer, and that bid is filled even when the quote is past 100 times. Other shelf goods refill when the pile is under half its target, taking the smallest lot that reaches the target, even if that lot runs past it: 0.5 t clothing, tools, leather, and textiles; 3 t meat; 5 t fish and grain; 10 t dairy, fruit, vegetables, and coal; 50 t wood. A workshop sells at least a ton of a shelf good to a market. A market buys only goods on its shelf. From 3 to 15 months of cover a price stays on the foreign list. A good with no local use is priced as if the town used one ton a year, and its quote stops at the foreign export price. Fish stops at $100, meat at $200, dairy at $30, vegetables at $35, fruit at $45, and hides at $700. The last ton of stock is left out of that cover. Below 3 months it can rise above the import ask when the year's import slots are full, up to $100,000. Above 15 months it can fall toward $1 when the export slots are full. Workshops and the stored town price follow that cover quote. The Economy tab lists a market's sale price beside it. A new workshop draws from the profitable specialties in `workshopOpening`. A blacksmith's margin is counted for three months of the year. The draw is 70% the best, 20% the second, and 10% another profitable specialty. A blacksmith is founded only when the owner still has at least $5,000 after buying the plot. Any other specialty needs at least $1,000. The workshop stays vacant when none is profitable or the purse is short. An occupied workshop keeps its specialty.
- **Climate presets, monthly temperature, and the monthly aridity used for salt.** `src/lib/climate.ts`. `monthAridity` is the monthly figure. `deriveClimate` is the yearly summary.
- **The map itself.** `generateWorld` in `src/lib/generate.ts`, run from `src/workers/map.worker.ts`. Leave this alone unless the terrain generator is the task.
- **Menus.** `src/components/ledger-panel.tsx`. Population, Education, Treasury, and Economy. Economy has Overview, Resources, and Classes.

**Files**

- `src/app/page.tsx` mounts the app. `src/app/layout.tsx` is the page shell.
- `src/components/planner-app.tsx` holds the game state and the Advance Month button. New rules have to be called from here or they never run.
- `src/components/setup-panel.tsx` is the founding form: climate, seed, map size, people, and difficulty.
- `src/components/map-pane.tsx` draws the map and sends paint and plop clicks.
- `src/components/building-bars.tsx` is the build toolbar.
- `src/components/inspector-panel.tsx` is the cell readout and the building card. The lines it shows come from `propertyLines`, `productionLines`, `tradeLines`, and `marketLines`.
- `src/components/weather-icon.tsx` and `src/lib/weather.ts` are the monthly sky and temperature on the header.
- `src/components/ui/` holds the buttons, tabs, and inputs. No game rules live there.
- `src/lib/types.ts` holds the map arrays: terrain, features, fertility, groundwater, and resources.
- `src/lib/colors.ts` holds the map colors.
- `src/lib/noise.ts` holds the seeded random numbers.
- `src/lib/run-generation.ts` starts a new map worker for each Found New State.
- `src/lib/utils.ts` is a small class-name helper.
- `scripts/verify-map.ts` checks map generation. `npm run dev` serves the app. `npm run verify` runs that map check.
