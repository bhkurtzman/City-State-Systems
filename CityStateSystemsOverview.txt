Reference this Guide for context regarding the long-term goals of CityStateSystems

Goals:
CityStateSystems is a working title for a strategy game prototype. My main goals are creating systems that work together and can be ported to a future desktop application.
 The game puts the user in charge of a newly founded micro-nation with a unique people. The goal is to create an accurate demographic, economic, cultural, logistical, and urbanist simulator.
 The player will be given a suite of statistics and policy nobs to create whatever kind of legal system, culture, and economy he wants.
 That said, the goal is for the simulator to work as a kind of ant farm, where the game can just run without much user input due to the dynamic and balanced systems.
 Realism is the goal: all citizen behavior, production and consumption, and economic activity should be based on reality.
 My medium term goal is for this grid/turn based game to run on a map-size of about 1000km2 with up to 100k population. Long term goal for eventual desktop app is 20,000km2 and up to 10m population.
 The game will also have a tech progress element. Your nation will research technologies at a variable pace which will unlock new buildings, efficiencies, resources, and policies.
 The game follows a simple philosophy that base extraction and especially agriculture are the foundation of civilization-- as a nation advances, agriculture becomes more efficient so more people may specialize.
 A key mechanic that is rather unique: demographics will play a big role-- Vigor, Intellect, and Willpower are heritable and marriage and fertility are dependant on the economy, culture, and laws of your nation. 
 Contrary to most city builders, increasing population through migration will be more difficult and have trade-offs concerning immigrant culture and attributes.
 A users country will be measured relative to other regions, which will follow a static progress and development schedule based on history. Depending on player decisions, your nation can be advanced or fall behind. Developed or undeveloped.
 
 Current State:
 This is a turn based game. Each turn represents one month. The map is 2d with 10mx10m tiles.
 The map and buildings on the map are also tracked-- the map has terrain features that influence buildings.
 Climate and map generation are set by the user. Climate affects map generation and agriculture.
 Each citizen is a unique agent with attributes, relationships, and information kept in a ledger that updates once per month.
 A citizen has parents listed. Siblings are citizens who share at least one parent.
 Siblings may not marry. A dead citizen is removed from the ledger once he has no living relatives (children, siblings, spouse). The ledger is rewritten when dead citizens are removed to maintain citizen paths.
 Citizens have 3 attributes that are partially heritable (genetically). Vigor, Intellect, Willpower. Each citizen has either level 1, 2, or 3 for each of these skills. Higher levels are typically better and bonuses are given to various probabilities depending on level.
 The basic unit is the family. Families form when a man and woman get married. A husband is the head of the family and keeps the family wealth in his purse. While a father is alive and under 65, his eldest son, that son's wife, and their children stay in his household. Children under 18 live with their family unless the parents have died. A younger son leaves when he takes a plot of his own.
 The game has three starting conditions, Warrior, Merchant, Explorer. Each have a unique attribute bonus, starting number of families, and treasury.
 A family (or single adult) will occupy a building and run it as an owner/occupier.
 A shack is a 1×2 house on prairie, scrub, or barren ground, and not on a mountain. It produces nothing. The click uses the cell to the east, or the cell to the south when the east pair is illegal. The household that owns it is unemployed and still buys food, fuel, clothing, and leather. A shack is priced as two urban tiles, offered after the other plots, and uses the small-farm purse rule.
 A construction office is a 3×3 state building. The player sets 0–10 laborers, in steps of 2. Each laborer supplies 100 man-hours a month, and unused hours are not kept. Each month it hires unemployed men aged 18–55 until the jobs are filled. Men already hired may stay past 55. A cut fires the newest hires the next January, after the December E1 wage is paid from the treasury. E1, E2, and E3 wages are set in the Government menu. A laborer is E1. The office keeps 0.1 t of tools and 0.1 t of leather, buying the difference in November when it is short. With at least one laborer it also keeps 50 t of lumber, 0.5 t of steel parts, and 0.2 t of textiles. It pays no more than twice the foreign price, remembers what it paid, and sells the pantry at a tenth over that cost with nothing held back. The payment returns to the treasury. A trading post or caravan house sells whole tons, so a small top-up from them is one ton. A building under construction buys what is already for sale, then the office refills, then the building can buy from that pantry. Lumber moves by the ton. Steel parts and textiles can move by the tenth of a ton. The tons still wanted, spread over three months, count as demand for imports and for workshop prices. A town price moves a tenth of the way toward its new level each month. In December, an office that worked that year rolls once per employee pair: 10% to use 0.1 t of tools and 5% to use 0.1 t of leather. A cart path is a one-tile road drawn in a straight north, south, east, or west line. It cannot cross water, mountains, or a building. A building or a farm tile cannot be placed on a path, planned or finished, and a farm will not fill in across one. A connected stretch is one project. The free office that would finish soonest takes it, and a smaller crew wins a tie. A tile is 10 man-hours, 15 on a hill, plus 10 for scrub, 20 for woodland, 50 for forest, and 30 for wetland. Woodland and forest are cleared to prairie and yield the same wood as a farmstead's clearing cut, 2 t and 5 t, into the office pantry. Wetland stays wetland and takes 1 t of wood. The office buys wood when clearing and the pantry do not cover that. After the project is finished, a market or workshop can buy the leftover wood. The treasury pays for the office's purchases.
 Farmsteads settle at 2–2,000 tiles and feature a single house. A larger outline can be drawn and filled, and it is outlined in yellow until it is cut down. Farmstead map tile composition affects farmstead production decisions. Each tile owned by a farmstead has a monthly production.
 Markets buy and sell domestically, taking a 10% (minimum $1) profit on all sales.
 Each good has a set foriegn regional price. This price is currently only set for western europe. The set foreign price is how much $ is exchanged for a given good. It is the same price for imports and exports. Any trading fees are separate.
 Trading posts export 1 ton of goods per month. A trading post can bring in 1 ton a month, and a caravan house can bring in as many tons as it is shipping that month. They rank an export by spare tons divided by the tons used in a year, counting a year that uses none as one ton. A good is offered only when that score is above 1.1 and the foreign price clears the fee. The roll is 40% for the highest score, 20% for the second, 10% for the third, and 30% for any offered good. A rank that is not on the list is drawn from that 30%. The tons come from one farmstead or workshop chosen at random among those with at least a ton to spare. That barn keeps selling whole tons until its spare falls under a ton, the good's score is no longer above 1.1, or the wagon is full. A roll with no such barn drops that good for every trader for the rest of the month and is tried again, up to three times. The next try is the qualifying good with the most stock, and if that also has no whole ton the next fullest is tried until one loads or none remain. If the wagon still has room, it rolls again. The seller is paid the foreign price minus the fee, and the post is paid the fee. An import is not bought ahead of a buyer. A market, workshop, sawpit, or construction buyer takes the cheapest tons it can get. Farm and workshop tons are priced at the town quote, a market shelf at its ask, and a trader's ton at the foreign price plus that trader's fee. A local ton is taken when the price is equal. When the cheap local tons run out, or the local price is higher, the buyer takes whole tons through the cheapest trader it can reach that still has inbound room. The buyer pays the foreign price plus the fee, and the trader keeps the fee. A buyer may use a trader it owns. A trading post is open to every buyer. A caravan house is open in a month it is shipping, and a buyer who has a workshop must be linked to that house. A good exported that month is not imported. Tons already sitting in the town pile stay for sale at their saved ask. A post takes one ton of grain in November and, in January, may use 0.05 t of leather, 0.1 t of textiles, and 0.1 t of steel tools.
 Trading posts collect a fee for imports and exports by subtracting a dollar amount from purchases for export and adding the same fee to imported goods that they sell. Each January the fee is rebuilt from the past year's average town price, using the foreign list when a good has no town price. Until that January it uses the opening prices. The bill is a year's supplies, the family allowance, and four urban tiles, raised by 10% and divided by eight tons, two thirds of the twelve it can ship. Supplies are 1 t of grain, 0.025 t of leather, 0.05 t of textiles, and 0.025 t of tools, at those yearly prices. The allowance is one cost of living, including its two urban tiles, plus six years of food, clothing, and leather. For the first five years after founding the post charges $65 a ton. After that the fee is $25 plus that bill per ton, and it is capped at $200.
 A caravan house is a 2×3 trading house. A family can buy it when a finished cart path runs from beside the house to the map edge, and the purse covers the land, 1,500 man-hours, 40 t of lumber, 0.4 t of steel parts, 0.1 t of textiles, and one operating year at double a post's supplies. Family food is not part of that test. The man-hours are paid to the treasury and worked by a free construction office. The family buys the materials and lives in the house while it is built. It ships only after both are done. With a sound road it moves 4 t each way, plus 2 t each way for each of up to two teamsters. A broken road is 1 t each way, plus 1 t for each teamster. The fee uses double those supplies, the same allowance, the January wage, and six urban tiles, raised by 20% and divided by two thirds of a year of that each-way capacity, then adds $15. It is capped at $200. During the year it changes only when the crew or the road changes how many tons that bill is spread over. A trading family is offered the house ahead of other buyers. The post's pantry is added to the caravan's, and the post is removed. It drops off the map at the start of the next month and is not offered for sale in between. The family eats from that pantry while the house is still being built. After twelve months on a sound road, January hires one teamster when outbound tons were at least 40, a second at 60, and drops the newest when the year falls under the line that hired him. The owner pays the E1 wage in December. Open seats are filled each month from unemployed men aged 18–55.
 Producers will not export their goods if the trading post fee exceeds the foreign value of the good, therefore some goods are not exported.
 A workshop is on the Production/Trade tab and has no placement limit. It takes 800 man-hours, 20 t of lumber, and 0.3 t of steel parts. A workshop or caravan house cannot sit on wetland or a mountain. Woodland on the lot adds 20 man-hours and 2 t of wood, and forest adds 50 man-hours and 5 t. That wood goes into the building's pantry when the clearing hours are done, and those tiles become prairie. Scrub, prairie, and barren need no clearing. The family can use the wood or sell it. The buyer pays the treasury for the man-hours and buys the materials, and must still have the specialty purse afterward: $1,000, or $5,000 for a blacksmith. The family lives there while it is built, and it produces only after both the hours and the materials are done. A workshop opens with an empty pantry. Starting stocks stay with the basic buildings. A sawpit is a basic 2×2, limited to one, and free to place. Its family is a craftsman and it runs the sawmill recipe from the month it is owned. It opens with the household pantry, including 20 t of wood, and 0.1 t of tools. Workshops specialize in one or a few similar goods. They require input resources, often fuel, and supplies, and they produce from a known recipe.
 Each person living in a building requires supplies to survive. Various foods, fuel, and some durable goods are needed. The cost of living is an estimate of this basket of goods.
 A household's cost of living is counted once in full. Every other person adds food, clothing, and leather at three quarters of the adult rate. The full cost is food, working-class clothing and leather, a year of wood or a quarter as much coal, and two urban tiles at the yearly lease or one twentieth of the sale price.
 All goods/resources are measured in metric tons. Buildings will not buy or sell less than 0.1T at a time.
 A household buys a year of fuel in the head's birth month, or in February when it produces wood, or in January when its kiln has made coal. It does not keep a fuel reserve. Clothing and leather stay in the pantry at 0.1 t, 0.2 t for an upper household, and 0.3 t for a wealthy one. Farms, workshops, and posts still keep operating stocks.
 Buildings can buy resources the same turn (month) they are needed.
 Citizens have a chance of illness or injury and chance of death from said illness or injury that rolls each month. Those chances change with citizen age and are affected by their traits.
 Education is currently 0,1,2. Illiterate, Primary, Secondary. Schools are not implemented yet but a set of rules for homeschool (passive) chance and graduation is already laid out.
 Buildings/property are placed by the user. The user can set a yearly lease or sale price per tile of a specific type. Once a building is leased, that lease price is maintained. A lease is generally multiplied by 20 to get an equivalent sale price. This is useful because a farmstead that contains multiple types of tiles can only be leased or sold as a whole.
 Vacant plots go to the wealthiest eligible buyer, one plot each. Buyers are household heads without a plot, and landless men of 18 who are not students and are not the eldest son still in his father's house. A small farmstead or a shack uses the old affordability rule, so a purse of $0 can take a plot priced at $0. A farmstead over 1,000 tiles, a market, a trading post, or a workshop also requires a purse of at least five times one adult's yearly cost of living. Shacks are offered after those plots.
 The UI has a top bar with map overlays on the left and Population, Education, Economy, and Treasury menus on the right. These have statistics as well as allow the user to set land prices.
 
 File Guide:
 The simulation lives in `src/lib`. The screen that calls it is `src/components/planner-app.tsx`. One Advance Month runs the steps in this order: `advancePeople`, `commitSettlement`, `settleHoldings`, `runConstruction`, `workFarms`, `tradeMonth`, `runRoads`.

People are columns of typed arrays. Money is whole dollars. Barn stock is hundredths of a ton. The same seed always rebuilds the same result through `hashSeed` and `mulberry32` in `src/lib/noise.ts`.

**Where to edit a mechanic**

- **Founding families, purses, and opening ages.** `foundPeople` in `src/lib/people.ts`. A warrior starts 20 families and adds $100 for each year past 18. An explorer starts 30 families and adds $60 for each year past 18. A merchant starts 10 families and adds $150 for each year past 25. Each family draws one surname. Stems and endings live in `src/lib/surname-banks.ts`. The draw, the join test, and `takeSurname` for a later immigrant household are in `src/lib/surnames.ts`. A wife copies her husband's id at marriage. A child copies the father's at birth.
- **Birth, death, aging, marriage, inheritance, epidemics, and the June school credits.** `advancePeople` in `src/lib/people.ts`. An eldest son still at home, or a man who owns a plot, may marry. Never-married women 18–45 may marry from any house. A widow under 45 may remarry only while she is still the head; widowers do not. A wife’s purse moves onto her husband. From a father's 65th birthday, or at his death, an adult eldest son takes the property. Half the purse goes to that son and half to the other sons, or to the daughters if there is no younger son. A father with no son retires onto his eldest married daughter when his last daughter marries, and the plot is sold back to the treasury. On the head's birthday, after that household has bought its supplies, a working father splits up to 20% of what remains among landless younger sons of 16, keeping one year of household cost. A son wealthier than his father is left out. A daughter's wedding draws one dowry of up to 10%, leaving this year's supply bill in the purse. From the fifth year, a household that still lacks grain, its garden choice, or protein after the bid is marked starving and tries the whole basket again six months later. A later miss rolls death at the next month, before marriage and inheritance: 50% at ages 0–5 and 65 or older, 30% at 6–12, and 25% at 13–64. A starving person does not marry or have a child. The household buys no fuel, clothing, or leather, and seeds no sons, until a food bid is filled. Food may be paid from any purse in the house, the head's first. A farmstead household does not starve.
- **Occupation.** `assignOccupations` in `src/lib/people.ts`. Student, then the job of the oldest house the owner or spouse holds, otherwise Idle under 18 and Unemployed at 18. A shack's job is Unemployed. A man on a construction crew whose house left him unemployed is a Laborer. A caravan hand in that position is a Teamster.
- **Homeless families and homeless single adults.** `residenceCensus` in `src/lib/people.ts`. A house is a farmstead, shack, market, trading post, or workshop that is already in place.
- **Classes, household cost, and who counts as one income.** `classCensus` in `src/lib/people.ts`. A couple costs twice an adult’s cost of living. The eldest son still at home and his wife each add a full adult, and their children add a full adult from 18 or half before that. Other children at home, adopted children, and retired parents add half. A single adult costs one, plus those same additions.
- **The income number itself.** `openIncomeMonth` and `noteOperatingIncome` in `src/lib/people.ts`. Sales, fees, goods bought, and leases are recorded. Land purchases, inheritances, and marriage transfers are not. The cash is tagged in `credit` inside `src/lib/trade.ts`, and lease payments are tagged in `settleHoldings` inside `src/lib/land.ts`.
- **Cost of living for one adult.** `costOfLiving` in `src/lib/trade.ts`. Grain, the fruit and vegetable average, and a year of protein weighted 10% meat, 45% fish, and 45% dairy. Dairy is 0.4 t. Clothing and leather use the working-class amounts. A full year of fuel and two urban tiles are added once per household. A good on a market shelf is priced at what the market is asking. A good with nothing on the shelf uses the town price.
- **Land prices, sale or lease, who is offered a plot, and the yearly lease bill.** `settleHoldings` in `src/lib/land.ts`. One plot per buyer. For the first ten years after founding, a market needs $2,000 and a post or large farm needs $1,000. After that, those plots need five years of one adult's cost of living. A workshop needs the land price, the man-hours, and the materials, and still $1,000 afterward, or five years of one adult's keep when that is more. A blacksmith still needs $5,000 left after the build. A shack is two urban tiles, needs no purse beyond the price, and is offered after the other plots. `plotSalePrice` is what the treasury pays to take a plot back. `monthlyCapital` is the trading-post fee’s land part, not a second rent.
- **Placing, deleting, and finishing buildings.** `applyBuild` and `commitSettlement` in `src/lib/buildings.ts`. Logging camps and mining camps are listed and disabled. A town can have one construction office, three trading posts, one market, and one sawpit. Workshops have no limit. A building marked for removal no longer counts, so another can be placed, and cancelling that removal is refused once the limit is full again. A shack is a green 1×2, east of the click or south when that pair is illegal, on prairie, scrub, or barren, and not on a mountain. The strip above the category bar names the selected building and its cost. A farmstead can be free drawn or stamped as a rectangle from the click, running south and east. The ghost is that rectangle and is not drawn above 2,000 tiles. The length boxes show the tile count and the hectares, at 100 tiles per hectare, in red when the count is over 2,000. A stamp leaves out road and building tiles. A fragment cut off by those gaps is dropped. The rest is one farmstead and merges into a farm it touches. A construction office is a yellow 3×3 on dry ground. A new farmstead over 2,000 tiles is dropped at the month advance. An established farm keeps its tiles and drops only the planned addition that pushed it over 2,000.
- **Construction crews and public wages.** `runConstruction` in `src/lib/construction.ts`. The Government menu sets E1, E2, and E3. Laborers are E1, paid in December. Hiring age is 18–55. January fires the newest extras. The office pantry is created empty in `workFarms`.
- **Caravan houses.** `src/lib/caravan.ts`, on the Production/Trade tab. A 2×3 house. Purchase needs a finished road to the map edge and cash for the land, the build, and double a post's operating year. The family pays the treasury for 1,500 man-hours, plus the woodland and forest on the lot, and buys 40 t of lumber, 0.4 t of steel parts, and 0.1 t of textiles. Clearing wood goes into the house pantry. Capacity is 4 t each way, plus 2 t each way per teamster, or 1 t each way plus 1 t per teamster when the road is broken. January hiring reads twelve sound months: 40 t outbound hires one man, 60 t hires the second, and a quieter year drops the newest. The owner pays the E1 wage in December. A trading family that buys one adds the post pantry to the house pantry, and the post is deleted.
- **Cart paths.** `paintRoad`, `assignOffices`, and `runRoads` in `src/lib/roads.ts`. A path is a straight cardinal line and cannot cross water, a mountain, or a building. A wooden bridge is painted the same way and only on sea or fresh water. Each tile must touch land on one of its four sides, so a crossing wider than two tiles is refused in the middle. A tile is 300 man-hours, 5 t of lumber, and 0.1 t of steel parts, taken from the office pantry when the tile finishes. The office orders whatever the unfinished bridge still needs beyond its pantry, and a shortfall under a ton still brings in one ton when the market cannot cover it. Finished bridge tiles count as road. Buildings and farm tiles cannot be placed on a path, planned or finished. One connected stretch is one project. Each laborer gives 100 man-hours a month. Hill is 15 hours, scrub adds 10, woodland 20, forest 50, and wetland 30. Clearing woodland yields 2 t and forest 5 t. Wetland takes 1 t of wood. November tops tools and leather up to 0.1 t. A staffed office also holds 50 t of lumber, 0.5 t of steel parts, and 0.2 t of textiles, paying at most twice the foreign price, and buys a whole ton from a post or caravan house. Construction bills and that pantry add to demand. Cover counts only goods for sale, so a reserve does not make a good look stocked. Prices glide by a tenth each month. December may consume them. Leftover wood can be sold once the office has no unfinished path.
- **Crops, farm yields, salt, farm coal, dried fruit, and opening barn stocks.** `workFarms` in `src/lib/produce.ts`. The good list and labels are `GOODS` and `GOOD_LABEL` at the top of that file. Household diet size is `familyPortions`. A farmstead's April fish haul stops at 15 t, and a sea tile adds salt in that same month. February, after the wood cut, counts prairie tiles and their average fertility and stores both. A new farm keeps the prairie count from when it is settled and chooses crops in February, so a farm settled after February grows nothing until the next February. Tiles added later wait for that count too. A garden draw is 40% fiber, 20% fruit, and 20% vegetables, and the climate crop takes the remaining 20% when it is one of those three. A vegetable or fruit tile yields 0.2 t at full fertility. At year 34 the orchard is cut for wood and planted again, and the farm's other crops stay. In June a vegetable farm with 5 t of vegetables, 0.01 t of textiles, and 2 t of wood turns them into 0.5 t of spices.
- **Workshops.** `src/lib/workshop.ts` and the batch constants and `runShops` in `src/lib/trade.ts`. On the Production/Trade tab, with no placement limit. Construction is 800 man-hours, 20 t of lumber, and 0.3 t of steel parts, paid like a caravan house. Woodland and forest on the lot add the same clearing hours as a cart path, and the wood goes into the workshop pantry. Wetland and mountain are refused. The pantry starts empty. Sawmill, blacksmith, smokehouse, creamery, and cooper. A creamery turns 8 t of dairy, 1 t of wood or 0.25 t of coal, and 0.02 t of salt into 1 t of cheese in March, April, May, September, October, and November. Cheese exports at $350. A cooper turns 2 t of wood and 0.2 t of steel parts into 1 t of barrels. A farm, workshop, or sawpit keeps one batch of each input. Inputs are bought in lots of a ton or more from a producer or a trader, and a smaller lot is used only when no ton is for sale. Supplies are bought in smaller lots when those are for sale, and in a whole ton from a producer or a trader when they are not. Barrels export at $400. A smokehouse uses 0.2 t of barrels for each ton of cured fish or cured meat. A specialty is rolled once per workshop in `produce.ts`.
- **Exports, imports, the trading-post fee, markets, the two-year reserve, and town prices.** `tradeMonth` in `src/lib/trade.ts`. Regional prices are `WEST_EUROPE`. The fee is `postFee` and `caravanFee`, locked by `rollTradingFee` each January from `tradingBill`. Prices average through `rememberFeePrices`. Supplies are priced by `operatingSupplies` at those yearly prices, the household by `familyAllowance`, then four tiles for a post or six for a caravan house. A post adds 10% and divides by eight tons, then adds $25. For the first five years after founding a post charges $65. A caravan house adds the January wage and 20% and divides by two thirds of a year of its one-way tons, then adds $15. Both are capped at $200. The export roll is `rankedExports` and `pickExport`. A good ranks by spare tons divided by yearly use, and a year that uses none counts as one ton. It is listed only when that score is above 1.1 and the foreign price clears the fee. One roll fills whole tons from one barn until the score, the barn, or the wagon runs out. A miss drops that good for every trader for the rest of the month and is rerolled up to three times, then the qualifying good with the most stock is tried, and the next fullest after that until one loads or none remain. Reserves are `stockTargets`. A household eats a year of food in the head's birth month, and burns a year of fuel then unless it produces that fuel. Wood is the fuel when the market ask, or the town price if the shelf is empty, is at most a quarter of coal. A market family takes that food and fuel from its own shelf at no charge, and buys only what the shelf does not cover. A food bid that finds nothing marks the household starving once the town is five years old. A short year does not. Meat, fish, dairy, fruit, and vegetables are swapped when the whole year is not for sale. The retry is the whole basket six months later, and a farmstead is never marked. Wood is taken in February. Coal is taken in January when the kiln has run. A farm eats a food it grows in that crop's harvest month and sells the rest. Fruit or vegetables is an even chance, or three to one toward the crop the farm grows, unless one costs more than ten times the other. Then the household takes the cheaper one. Food the household does not produce, and does not use in a workshop, is not kept. A remainder under 0.1 t is wasted. Clothing and leather roll on the head's birthday for everyone in the household: poor 2%, working 4%, middle 5%, upper 7%, wealthy 10% for 0.1 t of clothing. Leather for ages 13 and up is half that chance. Under 13, clothing is half and leather is not used. The pantry keeps 0.1 t of each, 0.2 t for an upper household and 0.3 t for a wealthy one, and the birthday buys the difference from the market only when a roll has drawn the pile under that amount. An upper or wealthy household also keeps 0.1 t of spices and, on that birthday, has a 3% chance to use 0.1 t. A farm's leather and textiles are a 5% chance of 0.1 t per 1,000 dry tiles. A workshop rolls tools at 5% and does not roll leather. A creamery also rolls textiles at 5%, and a cooper uses 0.2 t of fiber a year. Each farmstead keeps 0.1 t of tools, textiles, and leather, and each workshop keeps 0.1 t of tools. A creamery also keeps 0.1 t of textiles and 0.1 t of salt. A market opens at half of each shelf target, rounded to the nearest 0.1 t. Its shelf includes 1 t of salt. A buyer takes the smallest whole tenth of a ton from a market, or the smallest whole ton from anyone else, that reaches its target and then stops. A farmstead sells a protein only when that farm produces it. A farmstead sells at least a ton to a workshop at the town price, and buys only from a market in lots of at least 0.1 t. A post exports one ton a month from a farmstead or workshop with at least a ton to spare, chosen at random, and is paid the fee. The seller receives the foreign price minus the fee. A market, workshop, sawpit, or the construction office buys the cheapest tons first, and takes a whole import ton through the cheapest reachable trader with inbound room when local stock is short or priced higher. The buyer pays the foreign price plus that trader's fee. The trader keeps the fee. A good exported that month is not imported. A workshop and a caravan house deal with each other only when they share a side or the same finished cart path reaches a tile beside each. A sawpit is not under that rule. A market buys and sells with farms and workshops in lots of at least 0.1 t, at ten percent over what it paid. Meat, fish, grain, dairy, fruit, and vegetables are filled whenever the shelf is under half its target, unless the town quote is more than 100 times the substitute. Fruit pairs with vegetables. Meat, fish, and dairy each pair with the cheaper of the other two. Later in the year a market buys only enough of those to cover a buyer, and that bid is filled even when the quote is past 100 times. Other shelf goods refill when the pile is under half its target, taking the smallest lot that reaches the target, even if that lot runs past it: 0.5 t clothing, tools, leather, textiles, and spices; 3 t meat; 5 t fish and grain; 10 t dairy, fruit, and vegetables; 5 t coal; 50 t wood. A workshop sells at least a ton of a shelf good to a market. A market buys only goods on its shelf. Cover counts only what a buyer can take: goods above a reserve on a farm, workshop, sawpit, or market, spare wood at a caravan house, the construction office pantry, and tons already in an import pile. A reserve, and any barn that does not sell, stays out of that cover. From 3 to 15 months of cover a price stays on the foreign list. A good with no local use is priced as if the town used one ton a year, and its quote stops at the foreign export price. Fish stops at $100, meat at $200, dairy at $30, vegetables at $35, fruit at $45, and hides at $700. The last ton of what is for sale is left out of the price's cover. Tons already in the import pile still count that last ton. Below 3 months it can rise above the import ask when the year's import slots are full, up to $100,000. Above 15 months it can fall toward $1 when the export slots are full. Workshops and the stored town price follow that cover quote. The Economy tab lists a market's sale price beside it. A new workshop draws from the profitable specialties in `workshopOpening`. A blacksmith's margin is counted for three months of the year. The draw is 70% the best, 20% the second, and 10% another profitable specialty. A blacksmith is founded only when the owner still has at least $5,000 after the plot, the man-hours, and the building materials. Any other specialty needs at least $1,000 left after those. The workshop stays vacant when none is profitable or the purse is short. An occupied workshop keeps its specialty.
- **Climate presets, monthly temperature, and the aridity used for the April salt check.** `src/lib/climate.ts`. `monthAridity` is the monthly figure. `deriveClimate` is the yearly summary.
- **The map itself.** `generateWorld` in `src/lib/generate.ts`, run from `src/workers/map.worker.ts`. Leave this alone unless the terrain generator is the task.
- **Menus.** `src/components/ledger-panel.tsx`. Population, Education, Treasury, and Economy. Population has Overview and Wellbeing. Wellbeing counts starving households, the adults and children in them, and last year's starvation deaths. Economy has Overview, Resources, and Classes. Treasury ongoing expenses are construction-office salaries. Capital expenses are resource purchases. Capital income is land receipts, including leases and construction labor, plus resource sales. Each is the last twelve months, or the months so far. Tax income is empty.
